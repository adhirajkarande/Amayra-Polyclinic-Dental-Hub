UNDERSTAND THE PROBLEM

Why can wisdom teeth cause pain?

Wisdom teeth are the last molars to develop. When there is limited space or the tooth erupts at an unfavourable angle, it may become difficult to clean or partly trapped beneath the gum. This can lead to inflammation, decay, food trapping or problems affecting the neighbouring tooth.

Partially erupted tooth

A gum flap around a partly erupted wisdom tooth can trap food and bacteria and become inflamed.

Impacted wisdom tooth

A wisdom tooth may remain partly or fully trapped because of limited space or its position.

Decay or gum disease

Back teeth can be difficult to clean, increasing the risk of cavities and gum problems.

Pressure & irritation

An unfavourably positioned tooth can irritate nearby tissues or contribute to discomfort around the second molar.

Infection

Inflammation around a wisdom tooth may be associated with swelling, bad taste, discharge or difficulty opening the mouth.

Neighbouring tooth problem

Sometimes pain felt near a wisdom tooth comes from decay, a crack or another issue in the adjacent molar.

WHEN TO SEEK CARE

Wisdom tooth warning signs

  • Repeated pain or swelling at the back of the mouth
  • Food repeatedly getting trapped around the tooth
  • Bad taste, discharge or unpleasant mouth odour
  • Difficulty opening the mouth comfortably
  • Pain while chewing or swallowing
  • Decay or damage to the wisdom tooth or neighbouring molar
  • Fever or feeling unwell with dental swelling

Seek urgent care if swelling is spreading

Rapidly increasing facial or neck swelling, uncontrolled bleeding, major trauma, or difficulty breathing or swallowing requires urgent care.

Difficulty breathing or swallowing can be a medical emergency. Seek emergency medical care immediately.

DIAGNOSIS & PLANNING

How wisdom tooth problems are assessed

Clinical examination

We assess the tooth, gum tissue, neighbouring molar, bite and symptoms.

Dental imaging

When indicated, an X-ray or other appropriate imaging helps assess tooth position, roots, surrounding bone and nearby structures.

Treatment decision

Depending on the findings, options may include monitoring, managing inflammation, restoring a tooth where appropriate, or removal.

TREATMENT OPTIONS

Does every wisdom tooth need removal?

No. A healthy, functional wisdom tooth that can be cleaned and is not causing disease may sometimes be monitored. Removal may be considered when there is recurrent infection, significant decay, periodontal problems, damage to the neighbouring tooth, or an unfavourable position that creates ongoing problems.

After wisdom tooth removal

Recovery varies by case. Your dentist will provide instructions about oral hygiene, food, activity, medications when appropriate and follow-up. Contact the clinic if symptoms are worsening or you develop concerning swelling, fever or persistent bleeding.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about wisdom teeth

Why does my wisdom tooth hurt?

Pain may be related to partial eruption, impaction, inflammation, decay, food trapping or a problem in the neighbouring tooth.

Does every wisdom tooth need to be removed?

No. Removal depends on symptoms, disease, tooth position, cleaning access and the clinical assessment.

What are signs of an infected wisdom tooth?

Possible signs include pain, swelling, bad taste or discharge, difficulty opening the mouth and fever. Significant or rapidly spreading swelling needs urgent assessment.

Is wisdom tooth removal painful?

Local anaesthesia is used during the procedure. Some soreness and swelling can occur afterward, with recovery depending on the complexity of the extraction.

How much does wisdom tooth removal cost in Pune?

Cost depends on tooth position, whether it is impacted, imaging requirements and procedure complexity. An examination helps establish the appropriate treatment and expected cost.
