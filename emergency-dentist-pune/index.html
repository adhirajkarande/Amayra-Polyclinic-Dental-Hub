When Should You Seek Urgent Dental Care?

Not every dental problem is an emergency, but some symptoms need prompt professional assessment. If you are unsure, contacting a dentist is safer than delaying care.

Severe or Persistent Toothache

Strong pain, pain that is worsening, or pain that interferes with sleep or normal activities deserves dental assessment.

Dental Swelling

Swelling around a tooth, gum or face can be associated with infection and should be assessed promptly, especially when it is increasing.

Broken or Cracked Tooth

A fractured tooth may cause pain or expose sensitive inner tooth structures. Prompt assessment can help protect the tooth.

Knocked-Out Permanent Tooth

A completely displaced permanent tooth is time-sensitive. Seek urgent dental care and handle the tooth carefully by the crown, not the root.

Bleeding After Dental Treatment

Some oozing can occur after procedures, but persistent or heavy bleeding needs prompt professional advice.

Dental Injury

Sports injuries, falls and accidents can damage teeth, gums or supporting tissues even when the visible injury seems small.

What To Do While You Arrange Dental Care

01 · STAY CALM

Protect the Area

Avoid chewing on the painful or injured side and do not manipulate a damaged tooth unnecessarily.

02 · CLEAN GENTLY

Keep It Clean

For many dental injuries, gentle rinsing with clean water can help remove debris without aggressive brushing of the injured area.

03 · COLD COMPRESS

Reduce Swelling

If there is facial swelling after an injury, a cold compress on the outside of the face may provide temporary comfort.

04 · GET ASSESSED

Contact a Dentist

Arrange prompt dental assessment so the cause can be identified and appropriate treatment planned.

Seek emergency medical care immediately if you have difficulty breathing or swallowing, rapidly spreading facial/neck swelling, uncontrolled bleeding, loss of consciousness, or a serious head or facial injury.Dental care should not delay emergency medical treatment when these warning signs are present.

Frequently Asked Questions

What counts as a dental emergency?Severe or persistent dental pain, significant swelling, uncontrolled bleeding, major dental trauma and a knocked-out permanent tooth can require urgent assessment. Some situations also require emergency medical care.
Is a toothache always an emergency?Not every toothache requires emergency treatment, but persistent, severe or worsening pain should not be ignored. A dentist can identify the cause and advise how quickly treatment is needed.
What should I do if a permanent tooth is knocked out?Seek urgent dental care. Avoid touching the root, keep the tooth clean and handle it carefully by the crown. If appropriate and safe, ask a dental professional about the best way to preserve the tooth while travelling for care.
Can antibiotics alone cure a dental infection?Antibiotics are not a substitute for treating the dental source of an infection. A dentist should assess the problem and decide whether drainage, root canal treatment, extraction or other care is required. Antibiotics are used only when clinically indicated.
Can I wait if my dental swelling is painless?Do not assume painless swelling is harmless. Increasing swelling or swelling involving the face can indicate a significant dental problem and should be assessed promptly.
