UNDERSTAND THE CAUSE

Why does bad breath happen?

Most persistent mouth odour starts in the mouth, where bacteria can break down food debris and proteins. The important step is finding what is driving the odour so treatment addresses the source instead of simply covering it with mouth fresheners.

Gum disease

Bleeding, swollen or infected gums can be associated with persistent mouth odour and an unpleasant taste.

Plaque & tongue coating

Plaque around teeth and a coating on the tongue can harbour odour-producing bacteria.

Cavities & trapped food

Decay, food trapped around damaged teeth or poorly cleansed areas can contribute to mouth odour.

Dry mouth

Reduced saliva can make it harder for the mouth to clear debris and bacteria and may worsen odour.

Oral appliances

Retainers, dentures or other appliances need appropriate cleaning to avoid accumulation of debris and bacteria.

Non-dental causes

Some medicines, dietary factors and medical conditions can contribute. Persistent symptoms may need medical evaluation when a dental cause is not found.

WHEN TO GET CHECKED

When should bad breath be evaluated?

  • The odour persists despite regular brushing
  • Your gums bleed, swell or feel tender
  • You have cavities, tooth pain or food getting trapped
  • There is a persistent unpleasant taste in the mouth
  • You have very dry mouth or difficulty keeping your mouth moist
  • Bad breath is affecting confidence or social interactions
  • The cause remains unclear despite good oral hygiene

Don't just mask the smell

Mouth fresheners may temporarily reduce odour, but persistent bad breath is better managed by identifying and treating its underlying cause.

If bad breath occurs with facial swelling, fever, severe dental pain or difficulty breathing or swallowing, seek urgent care.

DENTAL ASSESSMENT

How we look for the source of bad breath

1. Dental & gum examination

Teeth, gums, plaque levels, gum pockets, restorations and areas where food may be trapped are assessed.

2. Oral hygiene review

Brushing technique, interdental cleaning, tongue cleaning, appliances and dry-mouth factors may be discussed.

3. Targeted treatment

Depending on findings, treatment may include professional cleaning, gum care, fillings or other dental treatment. Medical referral can be considered when appropriate.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about bad breath

What causes persistent bad breath?

Bad breath can be related to tongue coating, plaque, gum disease, cavities, trapped food, dry mouth or other oral conditions. Some cases have non-dental causes and may need medical evaluation.

Can gum disease cause bad breath?

Yes. Plaque and bacteria associated with gingivitis or periodontal disease can contribute to persistent mouth odour, especially when gums bleed, swell or feel tender.

How can I get rid of bad breath?

Good brushing, cleaning between the teeth, tongue cleaning, adequate hydration and regular dental care can help. Persistent odour should be assessed so the underlying cause can be treated rather than only masking the smell.

Is bad breath always caused by a dental problem?

No. Oral causes are common, but dry mouth, certain foods or medicines and some medical conditions can also contribute. A dental assessment is a useful starting point when the odour is persistent.

How much does bad breath treatment cost in Pune?

The cost depends on the cause and treatment required. It may involve professional cleaning, gum treatment, treatment of cavities or other dental care after examination.
