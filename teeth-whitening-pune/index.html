What Is Professional Teeth Whitening?

Teeth whitening is a cosmetic dental treatment designed to lighten the colour of natural teeth. It is different from cleaning: a dental cleaning removes plaque, calculus and some surface stains, while whitening aims to change the shade of the tooth.

Surface Stains

Coffee, tea, tobacco and some foods can cause external staining that may improve with professional cleaning and, when appropriate, whitening.

Natural Tooth Colour

Whitening may lighten the underlying colour of suitable natural teeth, although results vary from person to person.

Personalized Assessment

Existing fillings, crowns, veneers and some types of discoloration may not whiten like natural enamel and should be assessed first.

Who May Benefit From Teeth Whitening?

Whitening may be considered by adults who want a brighter smile and have suitable teeth and gums. The cause of discoloration matters when choosing the right approach.

Yellow or Dull Teeth

Some age-related or naturally darker tooth shades can respond to professional whitening.

Diet-Related Staining

Frequent tea, coffee and other staining foods or drinks can contribute to surface discoloration.

Event-Related Smile Enhancement

Some patients choose whitening before weddings, photographs, celebrations or other special occasions.

When Whitening May Not Be the First Step

Whitening is not appropriate for every type of tooth discoloration. A dental examination can help identify the cause and decide whether another treatment is more suitable.

Cavities or Gum Problems

Active dental disease should be assessed and managed before elective cosmetic treatment.

Existing Crowns or Veneers

Restorations generally do not change colour in the same way as natural teeth, so shade planning may be needed.

Deep or Internal Discoloration

Some intrinsic stains may respond differently and may require restorative or other treatment options.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is teeth whitening the same as dental cleaning?No. Cleaning removes plaque, calculus and some surface deposits; whitening is intended to lighten the colour of suitable natural teeth.
How long do whitening results last?Longevity varies with diet, oral hygiene, smoking or tobacco exposure and the individual response to whitening. Results may fade over time.
Can crowns and veneers be whitened?Whitening generally changes natural tooth colour, not the shade of existing crowns or veneers. This should be considered when planning cosmetic treatment.
Does teeth whitening cause sensitivity?Temporary sensitivity can occur with whitening in some patients. Your dentist can discuss ways to reduce the risk and manage sensitivity if it occurs.
Is whitening suitable for everyone?No. The suitability of whitening depends on oral health, the cause of discoloration, existing restorations and individual circumstances.
