CHILDREN'S ORAL HEALTH

Why children's dentistry is different

Children's teeth and jaws are developing, so dental care is not simply a smaller version of adult dentistry. Regular examinations can help identify tooth decay, enamel problems, bite concerns and oral hygiene issues early.

At Amayra Polyclinic & Dental Hub, we aim to make dental visits understandable and comfortable while guiding parents on brushing, diet, fluoride and habits that can affect developing teeth.

Preventive Dental Care

Regular examinations and age-appropriate oral hygiene guidance can reduce the risk of avoidable dental problems.

Fluoride Treatment

Professionally applied fluoride may be recommended for selected children based on their cavity risk and dental assessment.

Dental Sealants

Sealants can help protect the grooves of selected permanent back teeth where plaque can be difficult to clean.

Cavity Treatment

Early assessment helps determine whether a child needs preventive care, a filling or another treatment.

A SIMPLE APPROACH

What happens during a child's dental visit?

  1. Meet & settle in
    We give the child time to become familiar with the dental environment.
  2. Gentle examination
    Teeth, gums, bite and oral hygiene are assessed as appropriate for the child's age.
  3. Explain simply
    Parents and children are guided through what we find and what can be done.
  4. Prevention plan
    Brushing, diet, fluoride, sealants and recall intervals are discussed when relevant.

When should a child see a dentist?

  • For the first dental assessment after the first tooth appears or by around age one
  • If there is toothache, swelling or sensitivity
  • When you notice brown/white spots or visible cavities
  • After a dental injury
  • For advice about thumb sucking, pacifiers or other oral habits
  • For regular preventive check-ups as recommended

PARENT FAQ

Frequently asked questions about kids dentistry

At what age should my child first visit a dentist?

Early dental assessment is recommended after the first tooth appears or by around the child's first birthday. Earlier evaluation may be appropriate if there is pain, trauma or another concern.

Are milk teeth important if they will eventually fall out?

Yes. Primary teeth support eating, speech and normal development, and help maintain space for permanent teeth. Cavities and infection in baby teeth should not simply be ignored.

Does every child need fluoride treatment?

No. Fluoride recommendations depend on factors such as cavity risk, age, fluoride exposure and the child's dental examination.

What can I do to prevent cavities in my child?

Help your child brush effectively with fluoride toothpaste appropriate for their age, limit frequent sugary snacks and drinks, and maintain regular dental check-ups.

What if my child is scared of the dentist?

Tell the dental team about the concern before the visit. A gradual, reassuring approach and age-appropriate communication can make dental care easier for many children.

Should a child see a dentist after a fall or tooth injury?

Yes. Dental trauma can affect teeth and surrounding tissues even when damage is not immediately obvious. Prompt assessment is especially important if there is significant pain, bleeding, looseness or a change in tooth position.
