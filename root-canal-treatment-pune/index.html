Do You Need a Root Canal?

A root canal can help preserve a natural tooth when the pulp inside it becomes inflamed or infected. Common symptoms can include persistent toothache, sensitivity to hot or cold, pain while biting, swelling or a damaged tooth. Not every toothache needs a root canal, so a dental examination and appropriate imaging are important.

Root Canal Treatment, Explained Simply

1. Examination

Your tooth is assessed and imaging may be used to understand the source of the problem.

2. Comfort & Isolation

Local anaesthesia and appropriate isolation are used to keep the procedure controlled and comfortable.

3. Cleaning

The infected or inflamed pulp is removed and the root canal system is cleaned and shaped.

4. Sealing & Restoration

The cleaned canals are sealed and the tooth is restored to help protect it during normal function.

When Should You See a Dentist?

  • Severe or persistent tooth pain
  • Prolonged hot/cold sensitivity
  • Pain while chewing or biting
  • Swelling around a tooth or gum
  • A deep cavity, crack or dental injury
  • A tooth that has become discoloured or previously had treatment

Facial swelling, fever, difficulty swallowing or difficulty breathing can require urgent dental or medical attention.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is root canal treatment painful?

Modern root canal treatment is performed with local anaesthesia. Some tenderness can occur after treatment and should be discussed with your dentist if significant or persistent.

Can a root canal save my tooth?

When a tooth is suitable for treatment, root canal therapy can remove diseased pulp and allow the natural tooth to be restored.

Will I need a crown after RCT?

Many root-treated back teeth need a protective restoration, but the recommendation depends on the tooth and how much structure remains.

How many visits are needed?

It varies according to the tooth, infection, anatomy and treatment plan. Your dentist can explain the expected schedule after examination.
