KNOW THE SIGNS

What does a cavity feel like?

Tooth decay may develop silently at first. As the damaged area becomes deeper, symptoms can include sensitivity, food lodgement, pain or a visible change in the tooth. A cavity does not always hurt, so a painless tooth can still need treatment.

Cold or sweet sensitivity

Brief sensitivity to cold, sweets or acidic foods can occur as decay progresses into deeper tooth layers.

Food getting stuck

A cavity between or on the biting surface of teeth may repeatedly trap food and be difficult to clean.

Visible hole or dark area

Brown, black or chalky areas, pits or a visible hole may indicate tooth structure has been damaged.

Pain while chewing

Pain on biting can occur when decay is advanced or when the tooth has another structural problem.

Persistent toothache

Increasing or spontaneous pain can indicate that decay has progressed toward the pulp and needs prompt assessment.

No symptoms at all

Early decay can be symptom-free, which is why regular dental examinations can help identify problems before they become extensive.

WHY DECAY HAPPENS

Common causes and risk factors

  • Frequent sugary snacks and drinks
  • Plaque accumulation and inadequate cleaning
  • Not cleaning between teeth
  • Dry mouth or reduced saliva
  • Deep pits and grooves that retain plaque
  • Existing fillings or teeth that are difficult to clean
  • Irregular dental check-ups

Early treatment usually means simpler treatment

If decay is limited to the tooth structure, a filling may be enough. Waiting until pain becomes severe can mean the tooth needs more extensive treatment.

Facial swelling, fever, difficulty swallowing or breathing, or rapidly worsening pain requires urgent professional assessment.

DIAGNOSIS

How we check a cavity

Clinical examination

We inspect the tooth, neighbouring teeth and gums and assess visible changes, food trapping, sensitivity and tenderness.

Dental tests

Depending on symptoms, appropriate tests may help determine whether the decay is superficial or closer to the pulp.

X-ray when indicated

Radiographs can help identify decay between teeth or assess the depth of a lesion when it cannot be fully evaluated clinically.

TREATMENT

Cavity treatment depends on how deep the decay is

Preventive & early care

Very early enamel changes may be managed with fluoride, plaque control, diet advice and monitoring when appropriate.

Dental fillings

When a cavity has created a defect in the tooth, the decayed tissue is removed and the tooth may be restored with a suitable filling material.

Root canal treatment

If decay reaches the pulp and the tooth is no longer able to recover, root canal treatment may be recommended to treat the infected or irreversibly inflamed pulp.

Crown after extensive damage

A heavily weakened tooth may need a crown after appropriate treatment to restore function and protect remaining tooth structure.

Tooth removal when necessary

If a tooth is severely damaged and cannot be predictably restored, extraction may sometimes be considered after discussing alternatives.

Prevention after treatment

Restoring one cavity does not prevent new decay. Daily fluoride care, interdental cleaning, diet control and regular reviews remain important.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about cavities

What are the signs of a cavity?

Early cavities may cause no symptoms. As decay progresses, you may notice sensitivity to cold or sweets, food getting stuck, a visible hole or dark spot, pain or discomfort when chewing.

Can a cavity heal on its own?

Very early enamel changes may sometimes be arrested or remineralised with preventive care and fluoride, but an established cavity cannot be reliably repaired by home care alone and may need a filling or other treatment.

Does every cavity need a root canal?

No. Many cavities can be treated with a filling when the damage is limited. Root canal treatment may be needed when decay or other damage reaches the dental pulp and causes irreversible inflammation or infection.

How can I prevent tooth decay?

Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste, clean between teeth, limit frequent sugary snacks and drinks, maintain regular dental check-ups and address early signs of decay promptly.

What is the cost of cavity treatment in Pune?

Cost depends on the size and location of the cavity and the type of restoration required. A dental examination is needed to determine whether a filling or more extensive treatment is appropriate.
